Summary

Assuming \(\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N X_n \to 0\) a.s. for i.i.d. \(X_n\sim\mu\):

  • From \(\frac{X_N}{N}=\frac{S_N}{N}-\frac{N-1}{N}\cdot\frac{S_{N-1}}{N-1}\to 0\) a.s., we get \(\mathbb P(|X|\ge N\ \text{i.o.})=0\).
  • By Borel–Cantelli, \(\sum_{N\ge1}\mathbb P(|X|\ge N)<\infty\). The tail integral formula then gives \(\mathbb E|X|=\int_0^\infty\mathbb P(|X|>t)\,dt<\infty\), so \(X\in L^1\).
  • With \(X\) integrable and i.i.d., the (forward) SLLN says \(S_N/N\to \mathbb E X\) a.s.; uniqueness of the a.s. limit forces \(\mathbb E X=0\).

Why it matters.

  • Sharpness of SLLN assumptions. It shows the SLLN’s a.s. convergence cannot hold for non-integrable laws; integrability is necessary, not just sufficient, and the limit must equal the true mean.
  • Separates regimes by moment strength. \(L^1\) is the exact threshold for almost-sure Cesàro convergence; strengthening to \(L^2\) yields complete convergence (Hsu–Robbins–Erdős). This necessity-to-sufficiency ladder underpins modern refinements (e.g., hereditary/ subsequence forms of complete convergence).
  • Practical signal about heavy tails. Distributions with infinite first moment (e.g., Pareto with tail index \(\alpha\le 1\)) cannot satisfy the SLLN—sample averages won’t stabilize—so modeling and inference must change (truncation, winsorization, stable laws, or medians-of-means).
