You cannot have large fluctuations in a function without large gradients.

Today we had our first lecture of the summer. I must have read the

notes on the Poincaré Inequality
a hundred times before class, along with the
paper they were based on. Yet the lecture did not merely repeat the
derivations of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) semigroup and its infinitesimal
generator $L$.

Instead, the lecture invited us to wrestle with four foundational inequalities
in their most tractable setting: a quadratic potential and a linear drift.

After some formal algebraic manipulations, our professor introduced
us to the functional analogue of covariance, the Dirichlet form:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(f,g) \triangleq \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}} \langle \nabla f, \nabla g \rangle \, d\gamma
\end{equation}

and its self-interaction, the Dirichlet energy:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}(f,f) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}} | \nabla f |^{2} \, d\gamma
\end{equation}

We were reminded that the OU semigroup $ P_t $ satisfies:

\begin{equation}
\nabla (P_t f)(x) = e^{-t} P_t(\nabla f)(x)
\end{equation}

and by the commutation property, this extends to norms:

\begin{equation}
| \nabla (P_t f)(x) | \leq e^{-t} P_t(| \nabla f |)(x)
\end{equation}

Returning to the standard Gaussian measure $\gamma$ on $ \mathbb{R}^n $, we define the variance:

\begin{equation}
\text{Var}_{\gamma}(f) \triangleq \int f^2 \, d\gamma - \left( \int f \, d\gamma \right)^2
\end{equation}

The key ideas used in the proof (top of p.5 in the notes) are:

  • The ability to commute $\nabla$ and $P_t$, as shown above.
  • The invariance of $\gamma$ under the semigroup $ (P_t)_{t \geq 0} $.

So we prove: energy bounds variance. A famous inequality (Poincaré) now
has both analytic and physical meaning.

Even more astounding: from this, a cascade of seemingly unrelated inequalities
and information-theoretic identities emerge. Among them:

  1. Logarithmic Sobolev inequality, which controls entropy:

\begin{equation}
\text{Ent}_{\gamma}(f) \leq \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{| \nabla f |^2}{f} \, d\gamma
\end{equation}

  1. Talagrand inequality, which bounds Wasserstein-2 distance in terms of
    the Kullback–Leibler divergence:

\begin{equation}
W_2(\mu, \gamma)^2 \leq 2 \cdot H(\mu \Vert \gamma)
\end{equation}

We also encountered critical identities:

  • Fisher information:

\begin{equation}
I_{\gamma}(f) \triangleq \int \frac{| \nabla f |^2}{f} \, d\gamma
\end{equation}

  • De Bruijn identity, relating the rate of entropy dissipation to Fisher information:

\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt} \mathbb{E}[f_t] = - I_{\gamma}(P_t f)
\end{equation}

We only proved Poincaré today, but the others now come into view. Future
lectures will extend these results to Langevin–Smoluchowski dynamics.

A good proof should teach you something.
—Ioannis Karatzas

Today, my friends, we learned.
