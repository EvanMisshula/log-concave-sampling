Irreversible

Introduction

In stochastic processes, particularly for Brownian motion and diffusions, the strong Markov property (SMP) plays a critical role. While the forward process may satisfy the SMP, the time-reversed process often fails to do so. This document explains why—even for a diffusion without drift—time reversal may preserve the Markov property but not the strong Markov property.

We aim to give not just formal definitions, but the moral intuition behind this failure.

Forward vs. Reverse Time and Stopping Times

Forward Time: What Is a Stopping Time?

Let $ X_t $ be a Brownian motion adapted to the filtration $
\mathcal{F}_t $. A stopping time $ \tau $ is such that:

$$
{ \tau \le t } \in \mathcal{F}_t
$$

This means whether you’ve reached time $ \tau $ is a decision made
purely from the past and present.

  • Example: $ \tau = \inf{ t \ge 0 : X_t = 1 } $

Strong Markov Property (SMP)

Given a stopping time $ \tau $, the future of the process
satisfies:

$$
(X_{\tau + s} - X_\tau){s \ge 0} \text{ is independent of } \mathcal{F}\tau
$$

and behaves like a fresh Brownian motion starting at 0.

Key Assumption

The stopping time $ \tau $ must not “cheat” by looking into the
future.

Time Reversal: What Goes Wrong?

Define the Reversed Process

Let $ T $ be a fixed terminal time. Define:

$$
Y_t = X_{T - t}
$$

This is the time-reversed process. The reversed filtration is:

$$
\mathcal{G}t = \sigma(Y_s : 0 \le s \le t) = \sigma(X: 0 \le s \le t)
$$

What Happens to the Stopping Time?

Suppose we take $ \tau = \inf { t \ge 0 : X_t = 1 } $

Then in reversed time, the corresponding point is:

$$
\tau’ = T - \tau = \sup { t \le T : X_t = 1 }
$$

This is the last time $ X_t $ hits 1 before time $ T $.

The Core Problem

To know if a time $ s $ is this “last hitting time”, you must know
the future of the process (i.e., check that it never hits 1 again
after $ s $). Therefore:

  • $ \tau’ $ is not a stopping time in the reversed filtration $
    \mathcal{G}_t $.
  • You’ve violated the core assumption of SMP.

Moral Intuition

In forward time: “Stop the first time something happens”
$\leftarrow$ valid stopping time In reversed time: “Stop the
last time something happens” $\leftarrow$ depends on the
future

That is why the strong Markov property breaks:
the stopping time was fine going forward, but in reverse it
depends on what happens after.

Analogy for Learners

Imagine watching a video of someone walking on a chess board.

  • In forward time: “Stop the first time they step on a black square.”
    You don’t need to know the future.
  • In reverse time: “Stop the last time they step on a white square.” You
    do need to know they never step on red again—i.e., you use
    future info.

So even though the process may still have the Markov property under
reversal (memoryless transitions), the strong Markov property fails
because your stopping rule is no longer clean.

Summary

  • Markov property = future depends only on present.
  • Strong Markov property = true even at random stopping times.
  • Under time reversal:
    • The process may still be Markov.
    • But stopping rules (e.g., first hitting times) can become
      future-dependent.
    • Therefore, SMP often fails.
