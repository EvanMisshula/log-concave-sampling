Given a flow-induced transport plan between \(p_0\) and \(p^ *\), determining its optimality (in either Monge or Kantorovich sense) hinges on several factors. Let’s examine when such a plan can be defended as optimal, when it might not be, and under what conditions it could be far from optimal.

When Can It Be Optimal?

Quadratic Cost and Brenier’s Theorem

For the cost \(c(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} \|x - y\|^2\), the Monge problem is solved by a gradient of a convex function \(T = \nabla \varphi\) if:

  • \(p_0\) is absolutely continuous,
  • \(p^* \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)\),
  • The transport map is deterministic and invertible almost everywhere.

If the ODE flow induces such a map \(T\) from \(p_0\) to \(p^*\), with \(T = \nabla \varphi\) for some convex \(\varphi\), then it is optimal in both Monge and Kantorovich senses for quadratic cost.

When Is It Not Optimal?

In general, if:

  • The flow \(X_t\) is not a gradient flow,
  • Or the velocity field \(u_t\) is not the gradient of a convex potential,

then there is no reason to expect that the plan is optimal for any cost function — especially not for quadratic cost.

Is It Close to Optimal?

Even when the flow is not optimal, it may still be approximately optimal if:

  • \(p_0\) and \(p^*\) are close in Wasserstein distance (e.g., small variance),
  • The velocity field \(u_t\) stays close to a gradient field,
  • The mismatch between the induced displacement \(T(x) = X_1(x)\) and the optimal map \(T^*(x)\) is small in \(L^2(p_0)\).

When Would It Be Far From Optimal?

This is where it gets most interesting:

  1. Mismatched Geometry: If \(u_t\) does not respect the optimal transport geometry — for example, if it contains rotational or divergence-free components that cannot be captured by a Monge map.
  2. Multimodal targets: If \(p^*\) is multimodal and \(u_t\) tries to mix mass across modes without respecting convexity, it will be far from the optimal plan.
  3. High-entropy interpolation: If the intermediate distributions \(p_t\) are high entropy (e.g., due to injected noise), then the flow might prioritize entropy regularization over transport cost minimization — leading to Schrödinger-type transport, not Monge-optimal transport.

Answer witn minimal poetry

*“Does the flow induced by the ODE yield an optimal transport plan between \(p_0\) and \(p^\)?”**

Sometimes yes, if:

  • The flow map \(X_1(x)\) is the gradient of a convex function.
  • \(p_0\) is absolutely continuous.
  • You’re using quadratic cost.

Often not, unless:

  • The velocity field \(u_t\) is carefully constructed.
  • You’re in special cases like Gaussian \(p_0, p^*\) or linear score models.

But it can be close, especially:

  • When interpolations are near-Wasserstein geodesics.
  • In small-noise limits of Schrödinger bridge or score-based models.
