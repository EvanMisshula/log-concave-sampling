What is Log-Concave Sampling?

Log-concave sampling refers to the process of generating random samples from log-concave probability distributions—that is, distributions whose logarithm of the density function is concave.

Formally, a probability density function \(f(x)\) on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) is log-concave if:

\[ f(\lambda x + (1 - \lambda)y) \geq f(x)^\lambda f(y)^{1 - \lambda} \quad \text{for all } x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n \text{ and } \lambda \in [0,1]. \]

Equivalently, \(\log f\) is concave. Many important distributions-such as the Gaussian, uniform over convex sets, exponential, and Laplace-are log-concave.

Why is Log-Concave Sampling Important?

  1. Wide Applicability in Statistics and ML:

    • Log-concave distributions often arise in Bayesian inference, maximum entropy models, posterior distributions, and latent variable models.

  2. Efficient Algorithms:

    1. Log-concavity guarantees certain geometric and analytic properties—such as concentration of measure, unimodality, and convexity of level sets—which make sampling and optimization tractable. This allows efficient use of:

    2. Langevin dynamics

    3. Hamiltonian Monte Carlo
    4. Hit-and-run and ball walk methods
    5. Rejection sampling and adaptive MCMC

  3. Connections to Convex Geometry and Optimization:

    • Log-concave sampling is deeply connected to convex sets and convex functions, making it relevant in fields like high-dimensional geometry, stochastic optimization, and transport theory.

  4. Foundations for Advanced Algorithms:

    • Many cutting-edge results in differential privacy, robust estimation, and computational economics rely on the ability to sample efficiently from log-concave distributions.

  5. Theoretical Guarantees:

    • Algorithms for log-concave sampling often come with polynomial-time guarantees, making them attractive for large-scale applications.

Summary

  • Log-concave sampling is the backbone of efficient, high-dimensional inference and optimization.

  • Its importance stems from the structural simplicity of log-concave distributions and the wealth of tools from convex analysis, probability, and geometry that they unlock.

Log-Concave Sampling and Optimal Transport

1. Natural Geometry from OT

Optimal transport (OT) endows the space of probability measures with a *geometric structure*—especially under the 2-Wasserstein metric. This structure is well-aligned with log-concave measures because:

  • Log-concave measures minimize transport cost in many problems.
  • Many gradient flow dynamics (like Langevin diffusion) can be interpreted as Wasserstein gradient flows on the space of log-concave measures (Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto, 1998).

2. Wasserstein Barycenters

Computing barycenters (averages) of log-concave distributions under the Wasserstein metric involves:

  • Convexity in Wasserstein space,
  • OT geometry preserving log-concavity under specific conditions,
  • Applications in federated learning and generative modeling.

3. Sampling via Optimal Transport Maps

Sampling from a target log-concave distribution \(\mu\) can be reduced to transporting a simpler base distribution \(\nu\) (like Gaussian) via the Monge map \(T\) such that:

\[ T_{\sharp} \nu = \mu \]

If \(\mu\) is log-concave and \(\nu\) is Gaussian, the map \(T\) is the gradient of a convex function. This forms the basis of:

  • Implicit generative models,
  • Normalizing flows,
  • Score-based generative modeling.

See: El Moselhy & Marzouk (2012), Korotin et al. (2023).

Log-Concave Sampling on Manifolds

  • Log-concave sampling is most natural in Euclidean space, but many applications require sampling from distributions defined on manifolds:

1. Manifolds Arising from Constraints

  • Sampling on the sphere, Stiefel manifold, or simplex arises in directional statistics, graphical models, or latent variable inference.
  • These constraint sets often intersect with log-concave densities (e.g., log-concave on the sphere → von Mises–Fisher type).

2. Geometric Langevin and Riemannian MCMC

If the manifold is equipped with a Riemannian metric, log-concave sampling can proceed using:

  • Riemannian Langevin dynamics (Girolami & Calderhead, 2011),
  • Geodesic Monte Carlo,
  • Manifold MALA/HMC.

These methods adapt to curvature and geometry, crucial when working with embedded or constrained domains (like OT over simplices).

3. Entropic Optimal Transport on Manifolds

Recent work considers entropic OT between distributions on manifolds:

  • Combines Sinkhorn distances with Riemannian geometry,
  • Allows regularized and differentiable transport between log-concave measures.
