Introduction

As Leo Bao beautifully presented, from random velocity flips we arrive at a wave equation. I want to open with a quote that reminds us that mathematics, like art, seeks the essential. The Chinese say:

“咬文嚼字，不如得其神髓。”

``Rather than chew over every word, better to grasp the spirit.’‘

Leo did a beautiful job and I implore you to take the long pleasure of reading his excellent notes which are are available on our background page. I will try my level best to give you the highlights of what Leo did in depth and with grace.

First recall the telegrapher’s equation itself

$$ \begin{aligned} \partial_{tt} u(t,x) + 2 a \partial_{t} u(t,x) &= c \partial_{xx} u(t,x) \\ u(0, x) &= f_{0}(x) \\ \partial_{t} u(0, x) &= 0 \\ \end{aligned} $$

Leo then explains the relevance of the Hilbert Space metrics, probability space spaces with finite second moments, the Wasserstien-2 metric and the transport plan itself. Further, Leo introduces us to \(AC^2(I;\mathbb{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d))\) the space of absolutely continuous curves on an interval. Formally:

\[ AC^2\left(I;\mathbb{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d)\right) = \left\{ \mu_t : I \rightarrow \mathbb{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d) \,\middle|\, \exists m \in L^2(I) \text{ such that } \mathcal{W}_2(\mu_s, \mu_t) \leq \int_s^t m(r) \, dr \right\} \]

showing that both the velocity and conditional velocity field of the transport plan is a central insight of the paper. This firmly roots the paper in our reading groups focus on both optimal transport and sampling. Leo then reviews the notions of weak convergence before demonstrating the explosion of the transport plan velocity in diffusions. Leo follows the notation the paper our time runs from \(0 \rightarrow 1\) instead of the more standard \(0 \rightarrow T\).

Kac Flow in 1D

The next section begins with the careful construction of Kac Flow in 1D. He first starts the process at \(0\) then generalizes to \(X_{0}\). Leo then introduces the idea of weak differentiability intruduced Sergei Sobolev in the 1930’s and refined by Laurent Schwartz which I am sure will continue to be useful in the the study of Generative Processes which rely on time evolution to an invariant distribution and the reversal of such a process to generate new samples.

Leo states the explicit solution of the Telegrapher’s equation and and a weak distribution functions. This once again highlights the profound conection between measurable functions and probability distributions which we seem to discover again.

Kac Flow starting at \(\delta_{0}\)

Leo then shows a solution of the Kac Flow starting at a point process. Pushing forward, Leo show us how to solver in 1D for the velocity field of a 1D-Kac process.

$$ v(t,x) = \frac{x}{t + \left( \frac{r_{t}(x)}{c} \cdot \frac{I_{0}(\beta r_{t}(x))}{I'_{0}(\beta r_{t}(x))} \right)} $$

Extending Kac Flow to \mathbb{R}d

Leo methodically use independence arguments to extend the Kac Flow from one dimension to \(mathbb{R}^d\) before showing the diffusion and Telegraher’s solutions actually converges. However, the Kac has some notable advantages such as having an admissable transport plan where \((\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}})_{t \geq 0}\) is Lipschitz continuous in \(\mathbb{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^d)\) with constant $d^{\frac{1}{2}} c $. After which, Leo generalizes the Kac insertion method into \(\arrow{k}\).

This allows us to consider the (Conditional) Velocity Field of the Multi-d Wasserstein Flow

Here he shows us that in fact

$$ \mu_{t}(x) = - \sum_{i=1}^{d} \partial_{x^{i}}\left( \mu_{t}(x) v_{t}^{i}(x^{i}) \right) = - \nabla \cdot \left( \mu_{t}(x) v_{t}(x) \right) $$

In fact, once again showing the deep connection between the solutions of PDE’s and probaility:

$$ v_{t}^{i}(x^{i} \mid x_{0}^{i}) = \mathbb{E}[\dot{X}(t) \mid X_{t}^{i} = x^{i},\, X_{0}^{i} = x_{0}^{i}] $$

Finally he shows us we have what we need to define the loss function

Yes, it may have been bothering you that we don’t have access to \(v(t, x)\). However, the Corrallary 15’s Equation 39 shows us that the conditional vector field is all we need to find the velocity field:

$$ v_{t}(x) := \frac{1}{u_{t}(x)} \int u_{t}(x \mid x_{0})\, v_{t}(x \mid x_{0})\, f_{0}(x_{0})\, dx_{0} $$

Now you can create a loss function which any Neural Network can estimate the parameters for (or as our friends in CS say, ``it can learn the velocity field’‘. )

$$ \mathbb{CFM} (\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,T),\, x_0 \sim \mu_0,\, x \sim u_t(\cdot \mid x_0)} \left[ \left\| v^{\theta}(t, x) - v(t, x \mid x_0) \right\|^2 \right] $$

The work presented by Leo Bao highlights the deep connections between optimal transport, sampling, and partial differential equations. By deriving the telegrapher’s equation and exploring its properties, we gain insight into the behavior of stochastic processes and the underlying transport plans. The development of the Kac flow and its extension to higher dimensions provides a powerful tool for understanding the dynamics of these processes. Ultimately, this work enables us to define a loss function that can be used to estimate the velocity field, which is a crucial component in various applications, including generative modeling and sampling. By bridging the gap between optimal transport, PDEs, and machine learning, this research has the potential to drive advances in fields such as AI, statistics, and data science.
