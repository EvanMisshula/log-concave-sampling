When Is It Not Optimal?

In general, if:

  • The flow is not a gradient flow,
  • Or the velocity field is not the gradient of a convex
    potential,

then there is no reason to expect that the plan is optimal for any
cost function — especially not for quadratic cost.

Is It Close to Optimal?

Even when the flow is not optimal, it may still be approximately
optimal if:

  • and are close in Wasserstein distance (e.g., small
    variance),
  • The velocity field stays close to a gradient field,
  • The mismatch between the induced displacement and
    the optimal map is small in .

When Would It Be Far From Optimal?

This is where it gets most interesting:

  1. Mismatched Geometry: If does not respect the optimal
    transport geometry — for example, if it contains rotational or
    divergence-free components that cannot be captured by a Monge map.
  2. Multimodal targets: If is multimodal and tries to
    mix mass across modes without respecting convexity, it will be far
    from the optimal plan.
  3. High-entropy interpolation: If the intermediate distributions
    are high entropy (e.g., due to injected noise), then the
    flow might prioritize entropy regularization over transport cost
    minimization — leading to Schrödinger-type transport, not
    Monge-optimal transport.

Answer witn minimal poetry

*“Does the flow induced by the ODE yield an optimal transport plan
between and \(p^\)?”**

Sometimes yes, if:

  • The flow map is the gradient of a convex function.
  • is absolutely continuous.
  • You’re using quadratic cost.

Often not, unless:

  • The velocity field is carefully constructed.
  • You’re in special cases like Gaussian or linear score
    models.

But it can be close, especially:

  • When interpolations are near-Wasserstein geodesics.
  • In small-noise limits of Schrödinger bridge or score-based models.
