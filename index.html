Irreversible

Published: Fri 20 June 2025
By emisshula

In optimal transport.

tags: education mc

Introduction

Markov chains are a foundational concept in probability, used to model everything from weather patterns to web browsing behavior. One of their most elegant properties is reversibility — but not all chains have it.

In this post, we walk through a simple, non-cyclic, discrete-time Markov chain that is irreversible. That is, time has a direction in this chain — running it backward doesn’t produce another Markov chain.

This example helps build intuition for deeper topics like detailed balance, stationary distributions, and time-reversal in stochastic processes.

Defining the Chain

We consider a Markov chain with three states:

\begin{equation}
S = {0, 1, 2}, \quad
P =
\begin{pmatrix}
0.5 & 0.5 & 0.0 \
0.0 & 0.5 & 0.5 \
0.5 & 0.0 & 0.5
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

This transition matrix describes the following:

  • From state 0: move to 0 or 1 (equal probability).
  • From state 1: move to 1 or 2.
  • From state 2: move to 0 or 2.

There is no deterministic cycle, but the transitions bias flow in a
loop: 0 $\rightarrow$ 1 $\rightarrow$ 2 $\rightarrow$ 0.

Step 1: Finding the Stationary Distribution

Let $\pi = (\pi_0, \pi_1, \pi_2)$ be the stationary distribution. It
satisfies:

$$
\pi P = \pi, \quad \pi_0 + \pi_1 + \pi_2 = 1
$$

Solving the equations:

  • $\pi_0 = 0.5 \pi_0 + 0 \cdot \pi_1 + 0.5 \pi_2 \Rightarrow \pi_0 = \pi_2$
  • $\pi_1 = 0.5 \pi_0 + 0.5 \pi_1 \Rightarrow \pi_1 = \pi_0$
  • $\pi_2 = \pi_1 = \pi_0$

So all three components are equal: $\pi = \left(\frac{1}{3},
\frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}\right)$

Step 2: Check Detailed Balance

To be reversible, a Markov chain must satisfy the detailed balance condition:

$$
\pi_i P_{ij} = \pi_j P_{ji} \quad \text{for all } i, j
$$
Let’s check one pair: $i = 0, j = 1$

  • $\pi_0 P_{01} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 0.5 = \frac{1}{6}$
  • $\pi_1 P_{10} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 0 = 0$

These are not equal $\Rightarrow$ Detailed balance fails $\Rightarrow$ Not reversible.

Understanding the Markov Property

Even though the process is irreversible, it still satisfies the Markov property in the forward direction:

$$
\mathbb{P}(X_{t+1} = i_{t+1} \mid X_t = i_t, X_{t-1}, \dots)
= \mathbb{P}(X_{t+1} = i_{t+1} \mid X_t = i_t)
$$

This is true for any process defined by a transition matrix. So our chain is still a proper Markov chain in forward time.

However, the reverse-time process does not satisfy this property. The time-reversed process is not itself Markov — which is the hallmark of irreversibility.

Summary

✅ This chain is a valid Markov chain.

✅ It has a stationary distribution (uniform).

❌ It fails detailed balance.

❌ Its time-reversal is not a Markov process.

This makes it an instructive, minimal example of irreversibility
without cycles. You can think of it as a probabilistic ratchet: mass
flows preferentially in one direction, but not back.
